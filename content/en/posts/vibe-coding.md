I recently figured something out.

I used to think that once AI arrived, programmers would lose their jobs, product managers would lose their jobs, and everyone would lose their jobs. Later, I realized that AI can do many things, but it can do few things well. What makes the difference? **The human.**

---

## AI is smart, but it acts on the highest probability

I now use OpenClaw, Perplexity Computer, and several AI models together to write code. After using them for long enough, I noticed a pattern: AI really is smart, but it can only act according to established logic. It cannot truly discover requirements or design products. It only outputs what the large model considers the "highest-probability result."

That may sound like praise, but it is actually a serious problem.

Because **highest probability ≠ best solution**. Especially in product work, most people's needs do not match the mainstream expectations in the training data. AI will give you something that looks complete, standard, and "correct," but it is often just a pitch draft: nice to look at, useless in practice.

My solution is crude but effective: **first think the product through myself, then keep discussing the PRD, the spec, and even the technical implementation with AI until the idea is clear—then start building.** It sounds like one extra step, but it saves time. Rework is the real cost.

---

## The code will still have bugs

Even after I have used different large models for cross-auditing, problems still appear during real deployment. Sometimes an edge case was missed. Sometimes the context was misunderstood. Sometimes it is simply—who knows, maybe the model was in a bad mood that day.

This is where OpenClaw becomes valuable. It is like a tireless partner, helping me fix things layer by layer. I do not need to follow a process, wait for a development schedule, or manage anyone's emotions. I only need to say, "There is a problem here, take a look," and it goes to fix it.

Beautiful.

---

## What is vibe coding?

My interaction with AI is very simple and direct:

- I do not need to write a change process
- I do not need to care what time it is
- I do not need to care about the other side's emotions

I only need to raise the problem and solve the problem.

That is vibe coding. It is not a tool, but a working state: the human in the middle, AI on both sides, information flowing without friction.

---

## Where its real value lies

I do not think the greatest value of vibe coding is some grand narrative about "making programmers unemployed."

Its value is this: **it finally makes many small needs—needs too small to become commercial software—visible and solvable.**

You have an idea. You want to validate it, use it, own it. In the past, that meant learning a programming language, paying someone to build it, or simply giving up. Now? You talk with AI for half an hour, and it builds it for you.

That is why I like this technology.

Of course, I know there is still a long way to go. Whether AI can truly perceive and understand requirements by itself, I do not know. But honestly, I hope it cannot. That way I can still be useful.

---

Also, building new products is genuinely addictive.

I enjoy the process of discovering a need and trying to turn it into a product—even if it is a tiny need that maybe only I have. Turning an idea from zero into something real is addictive. Vibe coding makes that process lighter than ever, and makes that addiction feel within reach.

---

No matter how much it may look like one, AI will never be the end user.
